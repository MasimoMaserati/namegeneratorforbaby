50 Beautiful Irish Baby Names: Meanings and Origins

Irish baby names are loved around the world for their rich history, unique pronunciation, and strong connections to Celtic culture. Many names have been passed down for centuries, inspired by legendary heroes, powerful queens, ancient saints, and Ireland's breathtaking landscapes. Whether you're searching for a traditional Gaelic name or a modern favorite, these Irish baby names combine timeless beauty with meaningful stories.

Girls' Irish Baby Names

Aoife

Pronounced EE-fa, Aoife means "beauty" or "radiance." In Irish mythology, Aoife was a fearless warrior princess celebrated for her courage, intelligence, and strength. Today, Aoife remains one of Ireland's most popular girls' names and is admired for its elegant sound and deep cultural roots.

Saoirse

Meaning "freedom," Saoirse became internationally recognized through actress Saoirse Ronan, but the name has represented Irish independence and national pride for generations. Although its spelling may look unusual to English speakers, its beautiful pronunciation makes it unforgettable.

Niamh

Pronounced NEEV, Niamh means "bright" or "radiant." According to Irish legend, Niamh was the daughter of the sea god Manannán mac Lir and welcomed the hero Oisín to the magical land of Tír na nÓg, where no one grows old.

Maeve

Maeve comes from the legendary Queen Medb of Connacht and means "she who intoxicates." Known for her confidence, leadership, and determination, Queen Maeve is one of the most famous female figures in Irish mythology. The name has become increasingly popular around the world.

Ciara

Ciara means "dark-haired" or "dark." Dating back to Saint Ciara in the seventh century, this beautiful name combines simplicity with a long and respected history in Ireland.

Aisling

Meaning "dream" or "vision," Aisling became popular through Irish poetry during the eighteenth century. It symbolizes hope, inspiration, and imagination, making it a meaningful choice for many families.

Orla

Orla, also spelled Órlaith, means "golden princess." The name has royal origins and has been associated with Irish nobility for hundreds of years.

Clodagh

Named after the River Clodagh in County Waterford, Clodagh reflects Ireland's deep connection to nature. The name has a graceful sound while celebrating the country's beautiful landscapes.

Brigid

Brigid means "exalted one." It honors both the ancient Celtic goddess of poetry, healing, and wisdom, as well as Saint Brigid, one of Ireland's patron saints. Few Irish names have such a rich spiritual and historical background.

Éabha

Éabha is the Irish form of Eve and means "life." It has become increasingly popular among modern Irish families thanks to its elegant pronunciation and timeless simplicity.
