Irish Baby Names: Meanings and Origins

Irish names carry centuries of history, mythology, and Gaelic language roots, which is part of why they've stayed so popular well beyond Ireland's borders. Many trace back to ancient legends, early saints, or Old Irish words describing nature, strength, or beauty. Below are some enduringly popular choices, along with their meanings.

Girls' Names

  • Saoirse — Meaning "freedom." This name gained international recognition thanks to actress Saoirse Ronan, but it has been used in Ireland for generations as a symbol of independence.
  • Aoife — Pronounced EE-fa, meaning "beauty" or "radiance." Aoife was a legendary warrior queen in Irish mythology.
  • Niamh — Meaning "bright" or "radiant." Niamh was the daughter of the sea god in Irish legend.
  • Ciara — Meaning "dark-haired" or "dark." The name dates back to Saint Ciara of the 7th century.
  • Maeve — Meaning "she who intoxicates." Maeve was the legendary warrior queen of Connacht.

Boys' Names

  • Cian — Meaning "ancient" or "enduring." Associated with several figures in Irish mythology.
  • Finn — Meaning "fair" or "white." Most famously linked to the legendary hero Fionn mac Cumhaill.
  • Liam — A shortened form of William meaning "resolute protector." It is now one of the world's most popular boys' names.
  • Declan — Derived from an old Irish word meaning "full of goodness." Associated with a 5th-century Irish saint.
  • Oisín — Meaning "little deer." Oisín was a poet-warrior and the son of Fionn mac Cumhaill.

A Note on Spelling and Pronunciation

Irish names often use spellings that don't map directly onto English phonetics, so it's worth practicing pronunciation before settling on one. Names like Saoirse and Niamh are frequently mispronounced by those unfamiliar with Irish. If pronunciation ease is important to you, try saying the name aloud a few times—or ask an Irish speaker if you know one.
