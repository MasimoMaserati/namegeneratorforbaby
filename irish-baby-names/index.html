Irish Baby Names: Meanings and Origins Irish names carry centuries of history, mythology, and Gaelic language roots, which is part of why they've stayed so popular well beyond Ireland's borders. Many trace back to ancient legends, early saints, or Old Irish words describing nature, strength, or beauty. Below are some enduringly popular choices, along with their meanings. Girls' Names Saoirse — meaning "freedom," this name gained international recognition thanks to actress Saoirse Ronan, but it's been used in Ireland for generations as a symbol of independence Aoife — pronounced "EE-fa," meaning "beauty" or "radiance," Aoife was a legendary warrior queen in Irish mythology Niamh — meaning "bright" or "radiant," Niamh was the daughter of the sea god in Irish legend Ciara — meaning "dark-haired" or "dark," a name with roots stretching back to Saint Ciara of the 7th century Maeve — meaning "she who intoxicates," Maeve was a legendary warrior queen of Connacht Boys' Names Cian — meaning "ancient" or "enduring," a name associated with several figures in Irish mythology Finn — meaning "fair" or "white," most famously tied to the legendary hero Fionn mac Cumhaill Liam — a shortened form of William meaning "resolute protector," now one of the most popular names in the English-speaking world Declan — derived from an old Irish word meaning "full of goodness," associated with a 5th-century Irish saint Oisín — meaning "little deer," Oisín was a poet-warrior in Irish legend and the son of Fionn mac Cumhaill A note on spelling and pronunciation Irish names often use spellings that don't map directly onto English phonetics, so it's worth practicing pronunciation before settling on one. Names like Saoirse and Niamh, in particular, are frequently mispronounced by those unfamiliar with Irish. If pronunciation ease is a priority for you, it's worth saying the name aloud a few times — and maybe running it by an Irish speaker if you know one. Ready to explore more?
